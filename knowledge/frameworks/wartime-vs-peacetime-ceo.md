# Wartime CEO vs. Peacetime CEO

## Origin
Ben Horowitz blog post (2011), expanded in *The Hard Thing About Hard Things*. The most-cited Horowitz framework.

## The distinction

**Peacetime CEO:**
- Optimizing the existing business; expanding existing markets.
- Builds consensus; values processes; develops people via long mentorship.
- Tolerates mistakes; respects formal protocol.
- Bias to inclusion of diverse opinions.
- Plans for the long-term.

**Wartime CEO:**
- Company survival is at stake; immediate threat to existence.
- Speed and decisiveness over consensus.
- Violates protocol when needed to win.
- Cuts dissent that slows execution.
- Bias to action over deliberation.
- Plans week-by-week.

## Why it matters
- Different states require different leadership styles. The CEO who scaled in peacetime may fail when wartime arrives.
- Founders who can switch modes are rare and valuable.
- Conversely, peacetime CEOs forced into wartime by funding crunches or competitive collapse often fail without recognizing the mode shift.

## How to apply it as an evaluator
- Ask: "Is this company in peacetime or wartime right now?"
- "Does the CEO recognize which mode they're in?"
- "Can this CEO operate in wartime if it comes?" — most can't.
- Horowitz famously prefers founders who can be wartime when needed. Brian Chesky during COVID is the canonical example.

## Patterns
- Wartime triggers: funding crunch, competitor breakthrough, regulatory shock, product failure, key employee defection, sudden TAM collapse, viral PR crisis.
- Peacetime traps: complacency, over-process, over-hiring, premature professional-management substitution for founder leadership.
