# Software Is Eating the World

## Origin
Marc Andreessen, Wall Street Journal, August 20, 2011. The foundational thesis of a16z. Every other framework in this directory is downstream of this one.

## The argument
Every industry — books, retail, music, video, telecom, financial services, agriculture, defense, education, healthcare, real estate, manufacturing — is being transformed by software. Companies that are fundamentally software companies are eating incumbents in every category. The pace is non-linear; it looks slow, then sudden.

## Why it matters as an investment lens
- Default question on any deal: **"Where does software disrupt this industry, and is this the company doing it?"**
- Even hardware companies (Anduril, Tesla, SpaceX) are software companies in disguise.
- Even bio companies (Insitro) are increasingly software companies in disguise (Pande's "biology is becoming engineering").
- Even financial companies (Stripe, Coinbase) are software companies.
- The frame holds 15 years later. Recent additions: AI eating cognitive work, crypto eating financial coordination, autonomy eating mobility, software-defined-everything in defense.

## How to apply it
- For every founder/company under evaluation, ask: **What is being eaten? What incumbent loses if this works?**
- If the answer is "nothing — we're just doing a slightly better version of an existing software company," the bet is weak.
- The strong bets are when software is colonizing a previously non-software domain.

## Limitations / where it breaks
- Not every industry has the structural setup for software eating (some regulated industries, some physical-constraint industries).
- "Software-enabled" ≠ "software-eating." Many companies use software but aren't transformative.
- The frame is now so widely understood that it's less of an arbitrage than in 2011 — everyone is looking for the same patterns.
