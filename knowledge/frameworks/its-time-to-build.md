# It's Time to Build

## Origin
Marc Andreessen, a16z blog, April 18, 2020. Written during the early-COVID panic. Argued that the pandemic revealed America's loss of capacity to build physical things — masks, ventilators, vaccines, housing, transit, energy infrastructure.

## The argument
America forgot how to build. We have plenty of innovation in software bits, almost none in the physical world. Housing is too expensive because we don't build enough; education is broken because we haven't built new institutions in 50 years; healthcare is broken because we haven't built new delivery models. Public infrastructure is rotting because no one is permitted to build.

## The implication for investing
- The next decade of venture-scale value creation will be in builders of physical capacity.
- Defense, manufacturing, energy, housing, transportation, education, public health are all open for venture-funded innovation.
- a16z reorganized around this: **American Dynamism** is the direct operational descendant.

## How to apply it
- When evaluating: "Is this founder building a physical thing the country actually needs? Or another marginal software product?"
- Physical-world hardness is a feature now, not a bug. Mission gravity carries.
- DC fluency, hard-tech depth, dual-use commercial paths — all signals that align with the "build" mandate.

## Limitations
- Capital intensity of physical-world building is real; not every fund can absorb the timeline.
- Regulatory complexity in physical-world industries is no joke.
- "Builder narrative" can be co-opted by founders without real building chops.
