# Techno-Optimism

## Origin
Marc Andreessen, *The Techno-Optimist Manifesto*, a16z.com, October 16, 2023.

## The argument
Technology is the source of human flourishing. Tech progress is what created the modern world: longer lives, less poverty, freedom of expression, falling violence, expanded human capacity. The opposing worldview — "the precautionary principle," deceleration, sustainability-as-stasis — is a recipe for stagnation and decline.

## The manifesto's structure
- Lists what "we believe in": technology, markets, growth, intelligence, energy abundance, abundance, ambition, freedom.
- Names enemies: stagnation, the precautionary principle, regulatory capture, anti-merit ideology, "the credentialed gentry."
- Lists patron saints: Adam Smith, Friedrich Hayek, Filippo Marinetti, John von Neumann, Richard Feynman, and dozens more.
- Closes with a call to build.

## Why it matters as an investment lens
- It's now the firm's worldview-level filter. Founders aligned with this framing find easier alignment at a16z.
- Founders skeptical of techno-optimism (e.g., AI-safety doomers, deceleration advocates) find friction.
- Drives political positioning — anti-precautionary-principle regulation, pro-energy-abundance (including nuclear), pro-AI-acceleration.

## How to apply it
- Default to optimism about tech-driven futures.
- Skepticism of regulation as default; require strong specific arguments for any pro-regulatory position.
- Bias toward founders who are building, not warning.
- Bear case must be strong AND specific — generic anti-tech worries don't count.

## Limitations
- Risk of motivated reasoning — optimism can mask real risks (AI alignment, biosecurity, social fabric).
- The manifesto is genuinely polemical; not everyone the firm wants to back agrees with it.
- Can be alienating to founders whose worldview is more nuanced.
