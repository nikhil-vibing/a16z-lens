# Coinbase

## What it does
Crypto exchange (consumer + institutional), custody, staking, developer infra (Base L2), and an emerging payments / stablecoin platform. The largest US-regulated crypto company.

## Stage when a16z entered
Series A (~2013) and many subsequent rounds. a16z had one of the largest pre-IPO positions.

## Lead partner from a16z
- Founding-era: Chris Dixon was the primary champion.
- Ongoing: Dixon (board), Ben Horowitz (advocacy).

## Thesis bucket it fits
- **Primary:** [[crypto-onchain]] (flagship).
- **Secondary:** [[fintech]] (overlap).

## Why a16z backed it
1. **Brian Armstrong as founder** — ex-Airbnb, patient, missionary about crypto, willing to operate inside US regulatory framework.
2. **Regulated rails thesis** — Dixon bet that a compliant, US-regulated exchange would win the durable institutional relationship vs. offshore rivals.
3. **Onramps to the crypto economy** — Coinbase becomes infrastructure for everyone else: custody, identity, KYC, fiat<>crypto, developer tools.
4. **Cycle survival** — through 2014 bear market, 2018 bear, 2022 collapse-of-FTX bear — Coinbase kept building. Proof of long-arc operator pattern Dixon prefers.

## Trajectory / current state
- IPO'd April 2021 (direct listing, valued at $86B at peak).
- Survived 2022 crypto winter through aggressive cost cuts; came back strongly 2023-2024.
- Launched Base L2 (Optimism-stack rollup) in 2023 — now major source of dev activity.
- Strategically aligned with broader regulatory shifts post-2024.

## Lessons / pattern it sets
1. **Regulated > offshore wins long-term** — FTX collapse validated Dixon's pattern.
2. **Bear market builders are who you back** — Armstrong kept hiring engineers during bear markets while rivals shrank.
3. **Platform pivots compound** — Coinbase grew from exchange → custody → staking → L2 dev platform. Each layer reinforces the others.
4. **Founder political-engagement matters** — Armstrong publicly engaged the SEC fight; this set a tone for the industry.
