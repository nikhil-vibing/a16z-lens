# Airbnb

## What it does
Marketplace for short-term lodging (homes, apartments, rooms) plus increasingly experiences. Two-sided marketplace connecting hosts and guests globally.

## Stage when a16z entered
Series B and follow-on rounds (2011 onwards). Marc Andreessen joined the board.

## Lead partner from a16z
Marc Andreessen (board, long-running champion of Brian Chesky).

## Thesis bucket it fits
- **Primary:** [[consumer]] (marketplace, network effects).
- **Cultural value:** the canonical "founder mode" company — see [[founder-mode]] framework.

## Why a16z backed it
1. **Brian Chesky as founder** — designer-CEO with extraordinary attention to product detail.
2. **Two-sided marketplace with strong defensibility** — once a city's host supply is dense, it's nearly impossible to displace.
3. **Travel industry ripe for disruption** — hotels are operationally rigid; vacation rentals were a niche before Airbnb made them mainstream.
4. **Network effects across borders** — global travel demand + local supply.

## Trajectory
- IPO'd December 2020 at $47B; closed first day at ~$87B.
- Survived COVID near-existential crisis (March 2020 - mid 2021).
- Brian Chesky famously emerged from COVID with a "founder mode" management overhaul — eliminated traditional divisional structure, brought decision-making back to himself.

## Lessons / pattern it sets
1. **Founder-CEO with design taste** is a winning archetype.
2. **Wartime mode validates Horowitz framework** — Chesky's COVID response is now a Horowitz teaching case.
3. **"Founder mode" essay** by Paul Graham (Sep 2024) was largely about Chesky's Airbnb playbook — and a16z has embraced this framing strongly.
4. **Marketplace dominance is the durable consumer moat** — once you own supply density in a region, you compound.
