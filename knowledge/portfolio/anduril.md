# Anduril Industries

## What it does
Defense technology — drones, counter-drone systems, autonomous underwater/surface vessels, command-and-control software (Lattice), and increasingly large defense platforms (acquired Microsoft's IVAS / military AR headset contract). Software-defined defense at venture scale.

## Stage when a16z entered
Seed and Series A (2017-2018). a16z led/co-led early rounds.

## Lead partner from a16z
- Founding-era: Marc Andreessen and Katherine Boyle (post-2022 from a16z; Boyle had backed Anduril at General Catalyst earlier).
- Active board / partner involvement: Marc, Katherine, David Ulevitch.

## Thesis bucket it fits
- **Primary:** [[american-dynamism]] (flagship).
- **Secondary:** [[ai-infrastructure]] (Lattice is AI command-and-control), [[little-tech]] (the narrative archetype of small-company-out-innovating-Big-Defense).

## Why a16z backed it (cited reasoning)
1. **Palmer Luckey as founder** — proven founder (Oculus, sold to Facebook for $2B). Disruptive instinct. Politically incorrect — a feature for a16z.
2. **National-interest mission** — explicit patriotism; this isn't a financial-optimization play. Boyle's "founders who serve" pattern.
3. **Software-defined defense** — Anduril's product is fundamentally software-on-hardware, not the other way around. Marries a16z's software-eating-the-world thesis with the defense market.
4. **Anti-Lockheed/Boeing/Raytheon thesis** — the legacy primes are slow, cost-plus, anti-innovation. Anduril is fixed-price, fast, software-first.
5. **Procurement-as-customer thesis proven** — Anduril wins DoD contracts at venture-scale speed. The first proof that the AD thesis actually works.

## Trajectory / current state (as of 2026)
- Valued at $14B+ in latest rounds (mid-2024).
- Hundreds of millions in revenue.
- Defense-industry pole-position outside the legacy primes.
- Won major contracts: Replicator program (autonomous systems for Pacific theater), counter-drone, IVAS (transferred from Microsoft).
- Likely IPO candidate within a few years.

## Lessons / pattern it sets for similar deals
1. **Founders matter more than the market** — Palmer Luckey could have gone anywhere; he chose defense.
2. **Mission > margin** for at least the first 5 years. Capital efficiency comes later.
3. **DC fluency is a moat** — most VC founders can't navigate DoD; the ones who can have asymmetric upside.
4. **Software-defined hardware** is more venture-scalable than pure hardware OR pure software in defense.
5. **Politically incorrect founders** are not disqualifying — they're often the ones willing to build what others won't.
