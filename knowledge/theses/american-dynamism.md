# American Dynamism

## The thesis in one paragraph
American Dynamism (AD) invests in companies that support the national interest — defense, aerospace, public safety, education, housing, supply chain / manufacturing, and energy. The premise: decades of offshoring and bureaucratic ossification have weakened America's capacity to build physical things; the next great venture wave is rebuilding that capacity. Companies in this thesis serve both a commercial market and a civic / national-strategic function. Anduril is the canonical proof point.

## Why now (the inflection)

1. **Geopolitical realignment** — US-China competition, war in Ukraine, contested Pacific theater have forced acknowledgment that America has manufacturing and defense gaps.
2. **Cost-of-capital normalization** — patient capital is now competitive again vs. zero-rate consumer-software bets; hard-tech timelines are no longer fatally penalized.
3. **Software-eats-hardware maturity** — modern software (autonomy, AI, control systems) makes hardware companies more software-leveraged than ever; they look more like venture bets than industrial conglomerates.
4. **Founder culture shift** — a generation of mission-driven engineers (often ex-SpaceX, ex-Palantir, ex-defense-establishment) wants to build for the country.
5. **Government as customer maturation** — DoD's Defense Innovation Unit, OTA contracts, and similar mechanisms allow startups to win meaningful contracts faster than the old Lockheed-Boeing procurement timeline.

## Core sub-areas / investment surfaces

1. **Defense & national security** — drones, autonomous vehicles (air/sea/ground), missile systems, defense software, ISR (intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance), space-based capabilities.
2. **Aerospace** — satellites, launch, space logistics, hypersonics, advanced aviation.
3. **Supply chain & manufacturing** — precision manufacturing, supply chain visibility, reshoring infrastructure.
4. **Energy** — nuclear (fission small modular reactors + fusion), grid modernization, energy storage.
5. **Public safety** — first responder tools, emergency comms, border / immigration tech.
6. **Education** — alternative paths (apprenticeships, classical academies, microschools).
7. **Housing** — construction tech, modular building, zoning-reform-adjacent startups.
8. **Government tech / govtech** — modernizing federal/state/local digital infrastructure.

## Common founder profile a16z backs here
- Mission-driven, often ex-military, ex-Palantir, ex-SpaceX, ex-defense-industrial-base, or with deep civic motivation.
- Technical depth in their domain (a controls engineer for autonomy, an RF specialist for satellites, a manufacturing-process engineer for reshoring).
- Pragmatic about DC — knows how to win government contracts, navigate ITAR, get clearances.
- Often religious / patriotic / civic-explicit in their motivation.
- Comfortable in dual-use mode — building for government AND commercial.

## Anti-patterns — what AD is NOT investing in
- **Pure defense-software-as-SaaS** without dual-use commercial path.
- **Crypto/web3-defense crossovers** — off-thesis tonally and substantively.
- **"Smart city" generic IoT** without a real public-sector wedge.
- **Defense plays without a credible procurement strategy** — "we'll figure out the contracting later" is a kill signal.
- **Founders without national-interest mission story** — pure financial-motivated defense founders read as opportunistic, not credible.

## Open questions / where the thesis is being debated
1. **Procurement velocity** — can DoD actually move fast enough to give AD startups exits? Anduril's success suggests yes; the rest of the portfolio is testing the hypothesis.
2. **Political durability** — if administrations change, does AD capital stay favored? a16z has bet that this is bipartisan; mostly correct so far.
3. **Capital intensity** — many AD companies need >$1B to reach profitability. Is the LP base willing to wait?
4. **International expansion** — AD is explicitly American. Does the playbook export (UK, EU, Israel, India)?
