# Consumer

## The thesis in one paragraph
Consumer companies create products billions of people use to communicate, create, learn, play, transact, and connect. a16z's consumer practice bets on network-effect-driven businesses, creator economies, AI-native consumer products, and category-defining marketplaces. The lens is built on Andrew Chen's *Cold Start Problem* framework — atomic networks, tipping points, escape velocity, defensibility.

## Why now
1. **AI-native consumer products** are creating new categories (Character.AI-class, AI tutors, AI companions).
2. **Creator economy maturation** — Substack, Patreon, OnlyFans, TikTok Shop changed who owns audience.
3. **Mobile + global access** — Asia, Africa, LATAM consumer waves still unfolding.
4. **Saturation of old social** — Facebook/Instagram engagement plateaued; new attention surfaces emerging.
5. **Shifting consumer values** — younger consumers prioritize authenticity, niche communities, vertical media.

## Common founder profile
- Product-led founder with consumer-instinct.
- Growth-aware — has thought about acquisition channels and retention curves explicitly.
- Distribution-channel literate (TikTok / YouTube / influencer / SEO / app store).
- Has a clear "atomic network" thesis if it's a network-effect business.

## Anti-patterns
- **"Build it and they will come"** — no growth thesis.
- **Reliance on paid acquisition only** — economics break at scale.
- **Cargo-culted consumer mechanics** — copying TikTok's algorithm without TikTok's content.
- **Niche-to-niche pivots** without a coherent positioning.

## Open questions
1. **Is there room for new horizontal social platforms** post-TikTok, or only vertical ones?
2. **What's the durable AI consumer product** — companions, tutors, agents, or something not yet shipped?
3. **Mobile attention saturation** — what comes after the current scroll-feed paradigm?
