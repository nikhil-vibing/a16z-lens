# Little Tech / Builders

## The thesis in one paragraph
"Little Tech" is a16z's branding for *startups* — the small, fast-moving companies building America's future — as opposed to "Big Tech" (Google/Meta/Amazon/Microsoft/Apple) and "Big Government." The thesis is partly a political-economic argument (defending startups from regulation that benefits incumbents) and partly an institutional rallying cry. It's less a vertical thesis than a cross-cutting framing that informs every other thesis at the firm.

## Why now
1. **Regulatory capture risk** — proposed AI regulations (SB 1047 in California, federal AI executive orders, EU AI Act) advantage incumbents who can afford compliance.
2. **Political polarization on tech** — Big Tech increasingly seen as politically suspect by both parties; Little Tech offers an alternative narrative.
3. **Concentration of AI compute and talent** at frontier labs — Marc/Casado argue this is bad for innovation.
4. **a16z's strategic positioning** — by branding the firm as Little Tech's champion, a16z makes itself the natural counterparty to founders aligned with that narrative.

## What "Little Tech" advocates for
1. **Permissionless innovation** — startups should be able to build without preemptive regulation.
2. **Open source AI** — opposition to closed-model-only AI policy.
3. **Founder mode** — long-term founder-CEO control.
4. **Fewer barriers to entry** — antitrust enforcement of incumbents, not regulatory protection of them.
5. **National-interest building** (overlaps with American Dynamism).
6. **Anti-debanking** — opposition to financial-services-blocking of crypto and politically-disfavored startups (a16z fought this hard 2022-2024).

## Notable initiatives
- **Little Tech Agenda essay** (a16z, 2024) — founding document.
- **littletech.org** — dedicated content / advocacy hub.
- **DC policy office** — a16z has built a substantial DC presence.
- **Political endorsements** — Marc + Ben publicly endorsed Trump in 2024 partly on Little Tech grounds.

## Anti-patterns — what's NOT Little Tech
- **Companies dependent on incumbent partnership for survival.**
- **Founders agitating for regulation that benefits their own moat.**
- **Big-tech-incumbent-friendly policy positions.**

## How this affects investment decisions
- Founders aligned with this worldview have an easier time finding partner support at a16z.
- Founders publicly antagonistic to Marc / Ben / firm worldview face friction (rare, but it happens).
- Companies whose business model depends on regulatory moats are skeptically received.
