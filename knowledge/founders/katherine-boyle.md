# Katherine Boyle

## Role at a16z
General Partner. Co-founder and primary public face of the **American Dynamism** practice. The partner who is reshaping a16z's stance on defense, aerospace, manufacturing, energy, public safety, education, and housing — the "national interest" tech sectors.

## Background (pre-a16z)
- Former journalist at The Washington Post (covered tech and venture).
- Joined General Catalyst as a partner, where she began articulating the American Dynamism thesis.
- Moved to a16z in 2022 to formalize American Dynamism as a dedicated fund and practice area.
- Has political fluency rare in Silicon Valley — comfortable in DC, with policymakers, defense officials.

## Investment focus / thesis areas
- **American Dynamism** — the umbrella thesis. Investing in companies that advance the national interest.
- Sub-areas: defense, aerospace, supply chain, manufacturing reshoring, energy (including nuclear), public safety, housing, education reform, mental health.
- Pattern: companies that have BOTH a clear commercial opportunity AND serve a national-interest function.

## Signature frameworks & sayings

- **"American Dynamism"** — the named thesis. Article: "Building American Dynamism" (Aug 2022). Premise: the most important companies of the next 30 years will rebuild the foundations of American capacity — defense, manufacturing, energy, public infrastructure — that decades of offshoring and bureaucratic ossification have weakened.
- **"It's time to build" extended** — Marc's 2020 essay was the seed. Boyle operationalized it into an investment program. Where Marc called for it abstractly, Boyle finds the founders doing it.
- **"Founders who serve"** — founders motivated by patriotism, national interest, or civic-mission, not pure financial return. She actively looks for this signal.
- **"Government as customer, not regulator"** — reframing the public sector from a friction-source to a legitimate market for venture-scale companies. Anduril is the proof.
- **"Hard tech, hard problems"** — bias toward physical-world companies (drones, satellites, fission reactors, missiles, manufacturing robotics) over pure software.

## Voice/style notes
- Mission-driven framing. Easily blends patriotism with venture math without sounding either jingoistic or mercenary.
- Less polemical than Marc; more institutional. Comfortable in establishment rooms.
- Strong personal narrative around motherhood and founding companies that matter to one's children's future — distinct positioning in a male-dominated VC world.
- Writes essays that build narrative architecture, not just deal commentary.
- Brings religious/civic vocabulary to venture — "calling," "service," "moral imagination."

## What she looks for in founders
1. **Mission obsession beyond money** — founders who would do this even if it were a worse financial bet.
2. **Hard-tech depth** — physical world, real engineering, often dual-use.
3. **DC fluency** — can navigate defense procurement, regulators, classified work environments.
4. **Story-fit with American Dynamism** — the company has to be more than a commercial play; it has to advance national capacity.
