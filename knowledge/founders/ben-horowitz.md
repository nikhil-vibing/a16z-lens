# Ben Horowitz

## Role at a16z
Co-founder and General Partner. The operator voice of the firm. Where Marc owns worldview, Ben owns the lived experience of running a company at speed and scale. The "what to do when everything is on fire" perspective.

## Background (pre-a16z)
- Engineering / product roles at Silicon Graphics and Netscape.
- VP/GM at AOL.
- Co-founded Loudcloud (1999) with Marc Andreessen — pivoted to Opsware, sold to HP for $1.6B in 2007.
- Survived the dot-com bust running Loudcloud, came within weeks of bankruptcy multiple times. This experience is the source of *The Hard Thing About Hard Things*.
- Co-founded a16z in 2009 with Marc.

## Investment focus / thesis areas
- Enterprise software, leadership-heavy bets.
- Increasingly culture-focused bets: companies where the founder has a strong culture thesis.
- Crypto (along with Chris Dixon — supportive across the firm).
- Has been deeply involved in Coinbase, Lyft, Okta, GitHub, Slack, Foursquare, and many others.
- Personally interested in HBCUs, Black founders, and the political economy of tech — leads a16z's cultural/community work.

## Signature frameworks & sayings

- **Wartime CEO vs. Peacetime CEO** — a Horowitz original. Peacetime CEO: optimizing, growing, building consensus. Wartime CEO: company survival is at stake; speed and decisiveness over consensus. *Different rules for different states.* "Peacetime CEO worries about a big positive picture. Wartime CEO worries about a single big negative picture."
- **"There's no recipe for managing things you didn't expect"** (Hard Thing, opening chapter). The book's premise: the hard stuff has no template.
- **"Take Care of the People, the Products, and the Profits — In That Order"** — Hard Thing.
- **"Lead Bullets"** — there is no silver bullet; you ship better product, you out-execute, you grind. From Hard Thing.
- **The Struggle** — Horowitz's name for what every founder-CEO goes through emotionally. He's the only VC who writes about this honestly.
- **Culture is a competitive moat** — *What You Do Is Who You Are* (2019). Culture isn't a values poster; it's what people do when no one's looking. Drawn from samurai, Toussaint Louverture, Genghis Khan, and prison culture as case studies.
- **"Trouble is at the bottom of the stack"** — when a company is failing, look at culture and people, not product.

## Public writing / podcasts / talks
- ***The Hard Thing About Hard Things*** (2014) — the canonical operator's bible. Recommended reading for every a16z-eval'd founder.
- ***What You Do Is Who You Are*** (2019) — culture-as-system, drawn from non-tech case studies.
- **bhorowitz.com blog** — chapter-length essays from the Loudcloud / Opsware era. Most famous: "Lead Bullets," "Peacetime CEO/Wartime CEO," "Why You Should Train Your People," "The Struggle."
- **a16z Podcast** appearances — most insightful when interviewing operators and founders.
- **The Ben & Marc Show** — co-hosted firm podcast.
- **Twitter/X as @bhorowitz** — quotes hip-hop lyrics frequently; rarely combative; more philosophical than Marc.

## Voice/style notes
- Plain-spoken. Short sentences. Hip-hop lyric or military-history reference per essay, on average.
- Doesn't soften bad news. "Lay people off well." "Fire executives who can't perform."
- Genuinely empathetic about founder pain — he's lived the worst weeks of it.
- Often references close friends: Bill Campbell (the "Coach" of Silicon Valley), Andy Grove (Intel), Toussaint Louverture (Haitian revolution).
- Less political/polemical than Marc. Doesn't pick public culture-war fights.
- Strong on Black tech entrepreneurship, HBCUs, criminal justice reform. Personal commitments, not firm marketing.

## What he looks for in founders
1. **CEO who can keep going when the company is supposed to die** — the lay-off-everyone, almost-bankrupt, no-money-in-the-bank survival mode.
2. **Willingness to make brutal calls** — fire the friend, kill the product, cancel the offsite when the company needs the cash.
3. **Culture thesis** — not just a product thesis. What kind of company does this person want to build?
4. **Storyteller** — can the CEO recruit by telling the company's story compellingly?
