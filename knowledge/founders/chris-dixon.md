# Chris Dixon

## Role at a16z
General Partner. Founding partner of **a16z crypto** (started 2018; now manages multiple multi-billion-dollar crypto-dedicated funds). The intellectual leader of the firm's web3 / onchain / protocols thesis.

## Background (pre-a16z)
- Computer science background; co-founder of SiteAdvisor (acquired by McAfee, 2006).
- Co-founder of Hunch, a recommendation engine (acquired by eBay, 2011).
- Angel investor in Pinterest, Kickstarter, Stripe, Foursquare, etc.
- Joined a16z in 2012.
- Launched a16z crypto in 2018 — first major venture fund dedicated to crypto.

## Investment focus / thesis areas
- **Crypto / web3 / onchain economies** — the dominant focus since 2018.
- **AI** (increasingly — protocols + composability ideas extending to AI).
- Has called crypto "the most important computing trend since the smartphone."

## Signature frameworks & sayings

- **The Read / Read-Write / Read-Write-Own framework** — three eras of the internet:
  - **Read** (1990s): static pages, AOL / Netscape era. Information access.
  - **Read-Write** (2000s-2010s): social media, user-generated content, but value captured by centralized platforms (Facebook, Google).
  - **Read-Write-Own** (now): crypto enables user ownership of digital property — tokens, NFTs, onchain identity. Value flows back to users and builders.
- **The "Computer Next" framing** — every major computing wave (PC → internet → mobile → crypto) starts as a toy, ridiculed, then becomes the dominant platform. Pattern-match crypto to those earlier waves.
- **The Attract / Extract Cycle** — centralized platforms attract creators and developers with generosity, then extract value once they have lock-in (Facebook 2008-2012 vs. Facebook today). Crypto solves this with credible commitments via code.
- **Protocols > Platforms** — long-term, protocols (open, neutral, no rent extraction) outcompete closed platforms.
- **Builder-led, not investor-led** — crypto winners will be founder-defined, not VC-orchestrated.
- **Composability is a force multiplier** — onchain protocols compose like Lego; the platform's network effect compounds with each new integration.

## Public writing / podcasts / talks
- ***Read Write Own*** (2024) — book-length argument for the third era of the internet. The current canonical crypto thesis document.
- **cdixon.org** — earlier (2010s) essays on startups, network effects, "what the smartest people do on weekends is what everyone will do in 10 years."
- **a16z's "Future" essays on crypto** — including "Why Web3 Matters," "Three Important Lessons from Web3" series.
- **a16z Crypto Podcast** appearances.
- **Twitter/X as @cdixon** — measured tone, fewer culture-war pieces than Marc, more pattern-recognition essays.

## Voice/style notes
- Calm, professorial, persistent. Treats crypto as a settled long-arc question; doesn't get rattled by bear markets.
- Heavy on analogies — comparing crypto's 2018 to internet's 1995, or to the personal computer's 1977.
- Doesn't get pulled into short-term price/market drama. Focuses on builder activity, devs joining, infra maturing.
- Loves the "what the smartest people are doing on weekends will be what everyone does in 10 years" frame.
- Frames crypto as RESTORING the open internet that Web 2.0 closed off.

## What he looks for in crypto founders
1. **Technical depth** — protocol-level founders, not just app-layer "we put X onchain."
2. **Open-source / community-native** — willing to give up unilateral control for protocol legitimacy.
3. **Patience** — multi-cycle thinkers; not chasing one bull run.
4. **Builder-CEO** — same a16z pattern as elsewhere; the founder must keep building through bear markets.
