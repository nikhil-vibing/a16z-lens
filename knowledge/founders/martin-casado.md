# Martin Casado

## Role at a16z
General Partner. The senior infra/enterprise voice of the firm. The most influential a16z partner on questions of: open source business models, AI infrastructure, dev tools, networking, security, and the economics of platform businesses.

## Background (pre-a16z)
- PhD in computer science from Stanford.
- Worked at Lawrence Livermore National Lab on national security infrastructure (his early career carries genuine "served the country" weight).
- Co-founder of **Nicira** (software-defined networking) — sold to VMware for $1.26B in 2012, one of the largest enterprise networking acquisitions ever.
- General Partner at a16z since 2016.

## Investment focus / thesis areas
- **AI infrastructure** — model serving, inference layer, dev tools, post-training infra.
- **Open source businesses** — has the deepest published thinking in venture on how to make money from OSS.
- **Enterprise infrastructure** — networking, security, cloud, observability.
- **Defense / national security tech** — overlaps with American Dynamism; Casado's national-lab background gives him fluency.

## Signature frameworks & sayings

- **"Open source is not a business model"** — Casado's most-cited frame. OSS is a distribution / acquisition strategy. Monetization comes from: hosted/managed service, support contracts, premium proprietary modules, or owning the deployment surface. *Don't confuse adoption with revenue.*
- **The Cost of Cloud / Cloud Repatriation thesis** — co-authored "The Cost of Cloud, a Trillion Dollar Paradox" (a16z, May 2021). Argued large-scale companies pay too much for cloud relative to repatriating to bare metal. Massively influential, controversial.
- **"AI's economic constraints are different from SaaS"** — has repeatedly argued AI products have COGS that erode margins differently than SaaS, and that investors need new mental models.
- **"Defensibility comes from the deployment layer, not the model"** — for AI: foundational model layers will commoditize; durable margins live in proprietary data, distribution, vertical workflow integration.
- **"OS, distribution, defensibility — pick two"** — frequent open-source startup framing.

## Public writing / podcasts / talks
- **"The Cost of Cloud, a Trillion Dollar Paradox"** (a16z, May 2021, with Sarah Wang) — the most-cited and most-debated a16z essay of the 2020s.
- **"The New Business of AI"** — multiple essays on why AI economics differ from SaaS.
- **"The Empty Promise of Data Moats"** (a16z) — argued data alone isn't a sustainable moat in AI.
- **a16z Enterprise & Infra Podcast** — frequent host/guest.
- **Twitter/X as @martin_casado** — highly active, often debating AI-safety / open-source policy / cloud economics in public.

## Voice/style notes
- Engineer-first. Will write essays with charts, equations, unit economics.
- Doesn't moralize; argues from systems and math.
- Public defender of open-source against regulatory capture (SB 1047 in California, etc.).
- Pro-open-source AI, anti-precautionary AI regulation — close ally of Andreessen on AI policy.
- Less culture-war than Marc; more "this regulation is bad because it breaks this specific economic incentive."

## What he looks for in founders
1. **Real technical depth** — can the founder argue the system design with him for an hour and not blink?
2. **Clear-eyed about OSS monetization** — has thought about distribution AND extraction.
3. **Pragmatic about defensibility** — isn't relying on a magic moat; knows where the rent comes from.
4. **National-interest-adjacent if applicable** — same DC fluency as Boyle for defense bets.
