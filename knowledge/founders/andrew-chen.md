# Andrew Chen

## Role at a16z
General Partner. Consumer + growth specialist. The firm's canonical voice on network effects, viral loops, marketplace dynamics, and consumer scaling.

## Background (pre-a16z)
- Long career in consumer growth: led Rider Growth at Uber, advised dozens of consumer startups on growth.
- Built a massive audience as a growth writer (andrewchen.com newsletter, 100k+ subscribers, since 2007).
- Joined a16z in 2018.

## Investment focus / thesis areas
- **Consumer social / community / marketplace** companies with network effects.
- **Creator economy.**
- **Games + virtual worlds** (overlaps with a16z Games practice).
- **Network-effect businesses generally** — the lens applies to enterprise SaaS too.

## Signature frameworks & sayings

- ***The Cold Start Problem*** (2021, book) — the canonical text on how to start network-effect businesses. Frameworks:
  - **The Cold Start Phase** — getting the first atomic network to tip.
  - **The Tipping Point** — when a network becomes self-sustaining.
  - **Escape Velocity** — sustained growth that drives the network larger.
  - **Hitting the Ceiling** — saturation problems and how to extend.
  - **The Moat** — the long-term defensibility of mature networks.
- **The Atomic Network** — the smallest possible network that, on its own, is engaging enough to retain users. You build the company one atomic network at a time.
- **The "law of shitty clickthroughs"** — every growth channel decays over time as audiences become saturated. Growth = constantly inventing new channels.
- **The "next feature fallacy"** — believing one more feature will fix growth. Almost never true. The problem is usually distribution or network effects, not features.
- **Network effects compound; features don't.**

## Voice/style notes
- Empirical, data-driven. Lots of charts, frameworks, retention curves.
- Less polemical than Marc; less culture-war.
- Reflective, mentor-tone — writes for founders who are trying to learn, not VCs who are trying to look smart.
- Will give specific tactical advice: "your retention curve should look like this," "this growth loop dies at this scale."

## What he looks for in consumer founders
1. **Network-effect thesis** — what's the atomic network and how does it tip?
2. **Growth instinct** — does the founder understand WHY their growth works, or are they riding a temporary tailwind?
3. **Distribution channels** — multiple, layered, sustainable. Not "we'll do paid ads."
4. **Retention before scale** — does the product have organic retention BEFORE growth hacks?
