# Marc Andreessen

## Role at a16z
Co-founder and General Partner. The intellectual brand of the firm — sets the worldview that other partners apply in their verticals.

## Background (pre-a16z)
- Co-author of Mosaic (1993), the first widely-used graphical web browser, while at NCSA / University of Illinois.
- Co-founded Netscape (1994) — IPO'd 1995, marked the start of the consumer internet era.
- Co-founded Loudcloud / Opsware (1999, sold to HP in 2007 for $1.6B) with Ben Horowitz.
- Founded a16z with Ben Horowitz in 2009.

## Investment focus / thesis areas
Increasingly broad and worldview-level rather than vertical. Owns the firm's overall "software is eating the world" frame; involved in defining American Dynamism, AI, Little Tech, and the firm's political/regulatory posture. Personally led or championed: Facebook (board, sold position publicly known), Skype, Twitter, Airbnb, Pinterest, Stripe, OpenAI early stages, more recently Anduril and other defense/AI bets.

## Signature frameworks & sayings

- **"Software is eating the world"** (WSJ, Aug 20, 2011) — the foundational a16z thesis: every industry will be transformed by software. This single essay is the firm's North Star.
- **"It's time to build"** (April 2020 essay) — call to action post-COVID; argued America had lost its ability to build physical things and needed to rediscover it. Precursor to American Dynamism.
- **The Techno-Optimist Manifesto** (Oct 2023) — long-form declaration of beliefs: technology is the source of growth and prosperity; "we believe in technology"; lists enemies (deceleration, stagnation, sustainability-as-stasis, the precautionary principle).
- **"Strong opinions, loosely held"** → in practice he holds them tightly when challenged. Public debates with critics tend to be aggressive.
- **"The only thing that matters in a new market is getting to product/market fit"** (Pmarca blog, 2007).
- **"In a great market — one with lots of real potential customers — the market pulls the product out of the startup."** (Pmarca's "only thing that matters" essay.)

## Voice/style notes
- Long, polemical, list-form prose. The Techno-Optimist Manifesto reads like a constitutional preamble.
- Uses ALL CAPS for emphasis on X. "WE BELIEVE IN BUILDERS." This is intentional brand voice.
- Frames disagreements as civilizational, not technical. "Builders vs. those who would stop them."
- Will name enemies — by category (deceleration, regulators, "the credentialed gentry") if not by person.
- Loves historical analogies: Industrial Revolution, electrification, telecommunications. Tech progress as the through-line of human flourishing.
- Pro-immigration of skilled tech workers, pro-energy abundance (including nuclear), pro-space, pro-defense.

## Political / cultural positioning (as of 2026)
- Publicly moved rightward; supported Trump in 2024 election; endorsed JD Vance.
- Vocal critic of "the credentialed gentry" / mainstream institutional culture.
- Anti-DEI in tech corporate governance.
- Pro-crypto, anti-Operation-Choke-Point-2.0 (debanking) — has been a major a16z lobbying focus.
- This positioning is now part of the firm's brand. It's a feature for a class of founders, a deterrent for another.
